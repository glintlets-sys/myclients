Why Your Newly Renovated Bathroom Still Smells (And How to Fix It Permanently)

25 Aug 2026  |  EFCS Blog

You finally completed your bathroom renovation with modern tiles, stylish fittings, upgraded plumbing. Everything looks spotless.But then… a strange, unpleasant smell starts creeping in.You clean regularly. You disinfect. You even try air fresheners.Yet the odor refuses to go away.

If your newly renovated bathroom smells, the issue is rarely about cleanliness. In most cases, the problem lies deeper within the plumbing system, drainage design, or installation errors.

Let’s understand what’s really causing it  and how you can fix it for good.

What Causes That “Sewage” Smell in a New Bathroom?

The unpleasant odor you notice is often due to sewer gas entering your bathroom.

This gas is a mix of:

  • Methane
  • Ammonia
  • Carbon dioxide
  • Hydrogen sulfide (the main cause of the rotten egg smell)

A properly installed bathroom prevents these gases from entering through water seals in traps (like P-traps or floor traps).

When something goes wrong with these systems, the gas escapes into your bathroom — even if everything is brand new.

Common Renovation Mistakes That Lead to Bathroom Odor

Many bathroom smell issues start during renovation itself. Here are the most common causes:

1. Dry or Weak Trap Seal

Every drain has a water trap that blocks sewer gases.
If:

  • The trap is too shallow
  • Water evaporates quickly
  • The drain isn’t used often

…the seal breaks, allowing odors to escape.

2. Improper Trap Installation

If the trap is installed incorrectly or too far from the drain:

  • Water seal may not form properly
  • Gases can pass through easily

This is one of the most frequent post-renovation mistakes.

3. Poor Vent Pipe Design

Vent pipes help release gases outside.

If they are:

  • Blocked
  • Incorrectly installed
  • Shared improperly

…it creates pressure inside pipes, forcing bad odors back into your bathroom.

4. Faulty Toilet Sealing

During renovation, floor height often changes due to new tiles.

If the toilet isn’t resealed properly:

  • Gaps form at the base
  • Sewer gas leaks from underneath

Warning signs:

  • Smell near the toilet base
  • Slight toilet movement
  • Odor stronger at night or early morning

5. Early Biofilm Formation

Even new pipes can develop bacterial buildup within days.

This creates:

  • Damp, musty smells
  • Slippery residue in drains

Cleaning alone won’t fully solve this if the buildup continues.

Real-Life Situations You Might Relate To

✔ A bathroom smells every few days → trap drying out
✔ Everything looks perfect but still smells → sealing issue
✔ Smell comes after flushing → vent problem

These are extremely common and often overlooked during renovation.

How to Identify the Source of the Smell

Before calling a professional, try these simple checks:

✔ Run Water in All Drains

Let water flow for 20–30 seconds in:

  • Sink
  • Shower
  • Floor drain

If the smell disappears temporarily, your trap is drying out.

✔ Try the Oil Trick

Pour a small amount of mineral oil into the floor drain.
This slows evaporation and helps maintain the water seal.

✔ Check Toilet Stability

Gently push the toilet.
If it moves even slightly, the seal may be broken.

✔ Listen for Gurgling Sounds

Gurgling after flushing indicates vent pipe issues.

✔ Smell Individual Drains

If one drain smells stronger:

  • The trap may be faulty
  • Water may be siphoning out

✔ Consider Professional Inspection

If the issue persists, experts can use drain cameras to detect:

  • Pipe misalignment
  • Hidden gaps
  • Venting problems

How to Prevent Bathroom Odor After Renovation

Prevention is key to maintaining a fresh bathroom.

✔ Keep Traps Filled

Run water regularly in unused drains.

✔ Use Oil in Floor Drains

Helps maintain water seal for longer periods.

✔ Ensure Proper Installation

Always confirm:

  • Correct trap depth
  • Proper venting
  • Secure toilet sealing

✔ Clean Drains Monthly

Use hot water and mild cleaners to prevent buildup.

✔ Install a Trap Primer

A long-term solution that automatically keeps traps filled with water.

Why Professional Cleaning Still Matters

Even though most odors are plumbing-related, professional cleaning services play a crucial role in:

  • Removing biofilm and organic buildup
  • Maintaining hygiene in hidden areas
  • Preventing odor from returning

At EFCS, our expert cleaning solutions ensure your bathroom is not just visually clean — but hygienically fresh from within.

Final Thoughts

A newly renovated bathroom should feel fresh — not smell unpleasant.

If you’re facing persistent odor issues, it’s likely due to:

  • Trap problems
  • Venting errors
  • Poor sealing

The good news? These problems are completely fixable once identified.

Don’t ignore the signs early — addressing them quickly will save you time, cost, and frustration.

FAQs

Why does my bathroom smell even after deep cleaning?

Because the source is usually inside the plumbing system, not on the surface.

Is it normal for a new bathroom to smell?

No. A properly installed bathroom should not produce any odor.

Can the smell go away on its own?

Sometimes temporarily — but it will return unless the root cause is fixed.

When should I call a professional?

If the smell continues after basic checks, it’s best to get a professional inspection.
